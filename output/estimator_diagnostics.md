## Interpretation

Linear shrinkage reduced the condition number from 44.74 to 38.26, an improvement
of approximately 14.5%. It also raised the minimum eigenvalue, making inversion of
the covariance matrix more numerically stable. Nonlinear shrinkage reduced the
condition number by approximately 3.9% relative to the sample estimator.

All three unconstrained portfolios contain short positions, as shown by their
negative minimum weights and gross leverage above one. Linear shrinkage produced
the least negative minimum weight, lowest maximum weight, and lowest gross
leverage. It therefore moderated weight concentration, although the improvement
was modest.

The sample covariance matrix was not severely ill-conditioned in this application.
This is consistent with the relatively small number of assets compared with the
long estimation window and helps explain why the three portfolios performed
similarly.

## Suggested essay wording

> The JSE illustration provides modest evidence that shrinkage improves numerical
> stability. Linear shrinkage reduced the covariance matrix's condition number
> from 44.74 to 38.26 and produced slightly less extreme portfolio weights.
> Nonlinear shrinkage generated smaller improvements. The limited magnitude of
> the differences is consistent with the low-dimensional design, because
> shrinkage is expected to be most valuable when the number of assets is large
> relative to the available observations.
